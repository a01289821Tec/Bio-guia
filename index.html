🔬1. Cell Organization

🧫

Cell Theory

All living things are made of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life; all cells come from pre-existing cells.

🦠

Prokaryotic

No nucleus; DNA in the nucleoid. Small and simple (bacteria, archaea).

🧠

Eukaryotic

True nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (animals, plants, fungi, protists).

⚡

Mitochondria

Produce ATP through cellular respiration.

🏭

Ribosomes / ER / Golgi

Ribosomes make proteins; ER transports them; Golgi modifies, packs and ships.

🌿

Chloroplast

Site of photosynthesis in plant cells and algae.

🧬2. Introduction to Nucleic Acids

🧱

Nucleotide

Monomer of nucleic acids: a sugar, a phosphate group and a nitrogenous base.

🪢

DNA

Double helix, deoxyribose, bases A, T, G, C. Stores genetic information.

📨

RNA

Single strand, ribose, bases A, U, G, C. Types: mRNA, tRNA, rRNA.

1. ReplicationDNA → DNA
2. TranscriptionDNA → mRNA (nucleus)
3. TranslationmRNA → protein (ribosome)

Base pairing: A–T (A–U in RNA) and G–C. This is the Central Dogma of molecular biology.

⚠️3. Mutations

🔁

Substitution (point)

One base replaced by another. Can be silent, missense or nonsense.

➕

Insertion

Extra base(s) added. May cause a frameshift.

➖

Deletion

Base(s) lost. May cause a frameshift.

🧩

Chromosomal

Large-scale changes: duplication, inversion, translocation, nondisjunction.

TypeEffect
SilentSame amino acid; no change in protein
MissenseDifferent amino acid (e.g., sickle cell anemia)
NonsensePremature stop codon; shortened protein
FrameshiftReading frame shifts; usually a nonfunctional protein

Causes: copying errors, UV radiation, chemicals (mutagens). Only mutations in gametes are inherited.

🌱4. Inheritance

🫛

Gregor Mendel

Father of genetics; worked with pea plants.

🧬

Gene & Allele

A gene is a unit of heredity; alleles are its versions (dominant A, recessive a).

🎭

Genotype vs Phenotype

Genotype = allele combination (Aa); phenotype = observable trait.

⚖️

Homo / Heterozygous

Homozygous: AA or aa. Heterozygous: Aa.

Law of SegregationEach parent passes one allele per gene
Independent AssortmentGenes for different traits sort independently
Monohybrid crossAa × Aa → 3 dominant : 1 recessive (1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa)

🔄5. Cell Cycle

G1Growth, normal function
SDNA replication

MMitosis + cytokinesis
⏸️

Interphase

G1 + S + G2. The cell spends most of its life here.

🚦

Checkpoints

G1, G2 and M checkpoints verify DNA integrity and conditions before moving on.

🎗️

Cancer

Loss of cell cycle control causes uncontrolled division (e.g., faulty p53).

💤

G0

Resting phase; cell is not dividing (e.g., neurons).

✂️6. Mitosis

Produces 2 genetically identical diploid cells for growth, repair and asexual reproduction.

🅿️ ProphaseChromosomes condense; spindle forms
🅼 MetaphaseChromosomes line up at the equator
🅰 AnaphaseSister chromatids pulled apart
🆃 TelophaseNuclei re-form
🅲 CytokinesisCytoplasm divides

🥚7. Meiosis

Produces 4 genetically different haploid cells (gametes) through two divisions: meiosis I and meiosis II.

🔀

Crossing over

Prophase I: homologous chromosomes exchange segments.

🎲

Independent assortment

Metaphase I: random alignment of homologous pairs.

⚠️

Nondisjunction

Failed separation; causes e.g. Down syndrome (trisomy 21).

📝Self-Assessment Questions

1. What organelle makes ATP, and what organelle is only found in plant cells for photosynthesis?
Mitochondria make ATP; chloroplasts perform photosynthesis in plant cells.
2. Name the three steps of the Central Dogma and where each happens.
Replication (nucleus), transcription (nucleus, DNA → mRNA) and translation (ribosome, mRNA → protein).
3. Why can a deletion of one base be more harmful than a silent substitution?
A deletion causes a frameshift that changes every codon after it, while a silent substitution keeps the same amino acid.
4. In a cross Aa × Aa, what fraction of offspring show the recessive phenotype?
1/4 (25%), the aa genotype.
5. In which phase of the cell cycle is DNA replicated?
S phase of interphase.
6. What happens during anaphase of mitosis?
Sister chromatids separate and are pulled toward opposite poles of the cell.
7. Name two ways meiosis creates genetic variation.
Crossing over in prophase I and independent assortment of homologous chromosomes in metaphase I.
